How it works.

01

Find the experts

Quoted experts, their roles and affiliations are pulled from the article. Spokespeople and officials are set aside.

02

Confirm who they are

ORCID must return exactly one match for the name and affiliation, or you pick the right record. No match, no search.

03

Find disclosed ties

Exa searches research papers and Tavily searches the web. A tie counts only if a page names both the person and the organization.

04

Check the money

CMS Open Payments confirms payments from the same company to a physician with that name, shown with location so you can judge.

What it can't do.

Conflict Check is built to never accuse the wrong person. That means it says less, not more, when the evidence is thin.

Ties never disclosed

It finds connections disclosed somewhere else, in papers, bios and payment records. A tie that was never written down anywhere can't be found.

People with common names

If ORCID can't single out one person, nothing is searched until you pick the right record. Experts without a research record often can't be confirmed.

Payment records

CMS Open Payments covers US physicians only, and matches by name and location, not employer. It's shown as supporting evidence, never as proof.

Up to five experts

Each article is limited to five experts to keep searches focused. “No confirmed connections” is a normal, honest result.

Every quote is checked against the page it came from. No AI model states a connection.
